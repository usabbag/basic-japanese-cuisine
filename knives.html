Three knives

KnifeUse
Usuba 薄刃vegetables only; wide, rectangular; chips easily
Deba 出刃breaking down fish; itamae keep large, medium, small to match the fish
Yanagiba 柳刃sashimi and neta; finishing fillets; cutting the shari block. Up to ~36 cm (one shaku)

Single bevel

  • All three are kataba: flat on one face, angled on the other. The angled band is the shinogi.video 2-1
  • A single bevel drifts left when you push straight down; controlling that drift is the whole skill.video 2-1
  • Laid "flat", the edge of a single bevel lifts off the board on one side — flesh stays on the bone until you learn the true flat angle. A double bevel (ryōba) sits flat and cuts straight.video 2-1

Grip

  1. Middle finger on the heel (ago).video 2-1
  2. Thumb firmly on the heel, never floating.video 2-1
  3. Index finger on the spine (mine). Usuba only: index may instead pinch the blade with the thumb.video 2-1

This is the strongest hold — no wobble. Do not drift into a personal style.video 2-1

Whetstones

  • Lower grit = coarser. #220 brown stone: coarse (arato), for flattening and reshaping chips. Medium stone (nakato): the daily one.video 2-2
  • One working face only; never flip the stone.video 2-2
  • Soak until bubbles stop before every use; never dry.video 2-2
  • Rub the two stones together before and after sharpening to keep them flat; chamfer the corners — they cut.video 2-3
  • Stack the coarse stone under the medium to raise the working height.video 2-3

Sharpening straight edge, usuba

  1. Shinogi flat on the stone, knife at 30–45° to the stone. Normal grip.video 2-3
  2. Left index and middle fingertips on the shinogi, light pressure — pinpoint, fingertips only; a flat pad on the stone gets sharpened too.video 2-3·video 2-4
  3. Long strokes, forward and back, never short. Only the steel under the left fingers is being sharpened.video 2-3
  4. 30 strokes per position; work top of the edge to bottom in about five positions, until the left thumb meets the right. Add water when it runs dry.video 2-3
  5. Flip. Flat face (ura) on the stone, three left fingertips plus the right index on the maker's mark. Burr removal (bari-tori): top, middle, bottom of the edge, then pull through rotating clockwise. Three times.video 2-3
  6. Test: the edge should bite and stop crisply on the corner of the board; if it slides, sharpen again.video 2-3
  • Used knife: one set. Brand-new knife: three or four sets.video 2-4
  • Longer knife, more positions along the stone.video 2-5

Curved edges deba, yanagiba

  • The curved third (sori) sharpened straight up and down becomes straight. For that section move from the left corner of the stone to the right corner, wrist loose.video 2-4
  • Start at the tip; yanagiba curve is ~10 cm, three finger-widths at a time; past the curve, straight full strokes as usual.video 2-5
  • Yanagiba burr removal: four fingertips spread along the back, light and even; three passes, blade never leaves the stone.video 2-5

Finishing

  1. Polish the flat faces with a dab of polishing compound on a wet wine cork, on a board you don't mind dirtying. Don't touch the edge. Removes metallic smell and rust.video 2-3
  2. Rinse, wipe dry with paper towel pinched around the blade, never a hand over the edge. Into its case.video 2-3
  3. Flatten the stone in the sink, half then flip and rotate; flat when the two stones suck together and the #220 pattern is even across the face. Wrap in a wrung cloth, stack, store.video 2-5
