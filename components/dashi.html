Video says 20 g kombu for ~2 L and “a big handful” of bonito; Kansai uses more kombu, Kanto more bonito.video 9-2

Ichiban dashi

  1. Rinse the kombu quickly. Start in cold water. (Restaurants: in the fridge the night before.)video 9-2
  2. Heat to 65–70 °C and hold; kombu gives up most flavour there. Up to 30 min for thick kombu. Never boil. Lift the kombu out before the boil.video 9-2
  3. In class: at 80 °C (bubbles on the bottom) remove the kombu.
  4. Raise to 90 °C, add all the katsuobushi at once, go to ~95 °C, switch off.video 9-2 Steep 3 min — the video says ~10 min, until the flakes sink.video 9-2
  5. Strain through paper in a zaru. Do not squeeze — bitterness (egumi).video 9-2

Niban dashi

  1. Used kombu + used bonito in cold water, about half the first amount of water.video 9-2
  2. Medium heat to just before the boil; lift the kombu out alone (sliminess). Then low heat, gentle simmer 5 min. Optionally a second charge of fresh bonito (oigatsuo). Strain, no squeezing.video 9-2
