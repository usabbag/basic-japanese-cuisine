Tamago-dashi · Egg mix · Cooking · Cuts

Two steps: make a seasoned dashi (tamago-dashi, 玉子出汁 — the kitchen label calls it “omelet soup”), then beat it into eggs at a fixed ratio.

Tamago-dashi 玉子出汁 · “omelet soup”

Dashi ml

180 mldashi
40 gsugar
20 mlmirin
5 mlsoy sauce
5 gsalt

School card “TAMAGO Seasonings”. Class batch was 3× (540 / 120 / 60 / 15 / 15). One 180 ml batch yields ≈ 230 ml of tamago-dashi — enough for about 10 eggs at the ratio below.

Warm until sugar and salt dissolve, then cool completely before it meets the eggs. Keeps several days refrigerated.

Egg mix

Eggs

8eggs
180 mltamago-dashi (above)

Label on the kitchen bowl: “Omlet soup — 8 eggs / 180 ml”. Read as 180 ml of the seasoned liquid per 8 eggs (≈ 40 % of egg weight) — the sweet, firm Edomae-style tamago for nigiri. If instead the card's 180 ml of plain dashi were meant for 8 eggs, the liquid share would be ≈ 50 % (softer, closer to dashimaki). The bowl label is the clearer source; go with it.

Cooking

Procedure not recorded in class notes. Standard practice: beat the eggs with chopsticks, cutting the whites, no foam; stir in the tamago-dashi; strain through a fine sieve. Oil the makiyakinabe on medium, pour a thin layer, pop bubbles, roll while the top is still slightly wet, push the roll to the far side, re-oil, pour the next layer and lift the roll so it runs underneath, repeat until the mix is used. Shape in a makisu while hot; cool before cutting.

Cuts

  • 1 cm wide for nigiri.
  • 2 cm wide for appetizer, then cut in half.
  • Rabbit cut: 4 cm wide, pierce the middle, diagonal incision, flip 180°, again.
