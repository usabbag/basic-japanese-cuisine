Kongō dashi 混合出汁 · kombu + katsuobushi

Water L

2 Lwater — soft water is bestnotes
20 gkombu — 1% of the waterhandout
40 gkatsuobushi — 2% of the waterhandout

Video says 20 g kombu for ~2 L and “a big handful” of bonito; Kansai uses more kombu, Kanto more bonito.video 9-2

Ichiban dashi

  1. Rinse the kombu quickly. Start in cold water. (Restaurants: in the fridge the night before.)video 9-2
  2. Heat to 65–70 °C and hold; kombu gives up most flavour there. Up to 30 min for thick kombu. Never boil. Lift the kombu out before the boil.video 9-2
  3. Notes version: at 80 °C (bubbles on the bottom) remove the kombu.notes 21 Oct
  4. Raise to 90 °C, add all the katsuobushi at once, go to ~95 °C, switch off.video 9-2 Steep 3 minnotes — the video says ~10 min, until the flakes sink.video 9-2
  5. Strain through paper in a zaru. Do not squeeze — bitterness (egumi).video 9-2

Niban dashi

  1. Used kombu + used bonito in cold water, about half the first amount of water.video 9-2
  2. Medium heat to just before the boil; lift the kombu out alone (sliminess). Then low heat, gentle simmer 5 min. Optionally a second charge of fresh bonito (oigatsuo). Strain, no squeezing.video 9-2

Ni-shiitake 煮椎茸 · simmered shiitake

Dried shiitake g

250 gdried shiitake
540 mlshiitake soaking water
360 mlwater
360 mldark soy
500 gsugar
45 mlmirin
  1. Soak in water overnight.board Strain the soaking liquid through paper; it's the stock.video 9-3
  2. Seasonings into the soaking liquid, add shiitake. Skim the scum that rises at the first boil only.video 9-3
  3. Simmer on medium, thick-bottomed pot, until the liquid is gone — 60 min.board · video 9-3
  4. At the end drizzle mirin over to glaze, one more blast of heat. Cool on a flat bamboo tray.video 9-3

For chirashi and futomaki. Freezes well in portions; move to the fridge the day before use.video 9-3

Gari ガリ · pickled ginger

New ginger g

350 gnew (young) ginger
200 ccwater
100 ccvinegar
50 gsugar
10 gsalt
  1. Young ginger; old ginger is spicier and harder. Peel with a spoon; trim the pink offshoots and dry ends.notes 20 Oct
  2. Slice parallel to the fibres, 1 mm.notes
  3. 3% salt water, 10 min.handout · notes
  4. Drain; boil in plain water 5 min (counted from the return to the boil).handout · notes
  5. Marinate in the vinegar liquid.handout
  • Keeps 1 month in water, 1 year in vinegar.notes
  • Same method works with green papaya / palm heart.notes

Tsuma つま · sashimi garnish

Daikon

  1. Usuba grip: middle, ring and little fingers hold; index on the spine when cutting, on the side when peeling.video 7-1
  2. Katsura-muki: first peel thick to make a clean cylinder (mentori), then peel thin. Thumb is the pivot; knife moves up and down; cut only on the upward stroke. Eyes on the top edge of the daikon.video 7-1
  3. Roll the sheet, fold in half, trim the sides, julienne. Knife moves diagonally forward, not straight down.video 7-1
  4. Rinse in running water — removes the smell, makes it crisp.video 7-1 · 8-1
  5. Store wrapped in a wrung-out wet paper towel, in a bag or lidded container, fridge.video 8-1 · 7-2

Cucumber, carrot

  • Cucumber: katsura-muki using only the layer between skin and seeds; julienne with the daikon for two-colour tsuma.video 7-2
  • Yori-ninjin: carrot sheet cut diagonally in 2–3 mm strips, optionally two slits down the middle, wrapped round a chopstick to twist.video 7-2
